## Background

A partition of a set is a family of non-empty, pairwise disjoint parts that
jointly cover it, two partitions being identified when they have the same
blocks; partitions are interchangeable with equivalence relations, and equally
with surjections out of the set ([nLab: partition](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/partition),
[Wikipedia: Partition of a set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_a_set)).
This is the vocabulary the whole of Seven Sketches Chapter 1 is built on — the
chapter's running "systems" are partitions, and its order, join, observation and
Galois connections are all defined on them
([nLab: equivalence relation](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/equivalence+relation)).

## Background

The partitions of a fixed set form a lattice under coarsening: one partition is
below another when every block of the first sits inside a block of the second,
and the join of two partitions is the equivalence relation generated by the
union of their two relations ([nLab: partition](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/partition),
which treats the lattice of partitions of a finite set;
[nLab: lattice](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/lattice)). Seven Sketches runs its
whole generative-effects story on this lattice: the join is characterised by its
universal property as a least upper bound, and the connectivity observation into
the two-element order is the order-preserving map whose failure to preserve
joins the book is after.

## Background

diagram ([Wikipedia: Hasse diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasse_diagram)).

## Background

inclusion to the naturals ordered by size: an inclusion of subsets forces an

the book's first monotone map whose domain is itself a constructed order
([nLab: power set](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/power+set)).

## Background

total and single-valued — and then defines the preimage of an ELEMENT, the fibre,

relational presentation matters for the book because a displayed relation can then
be tested for being a function at all, which is what the accompanying exercise
asks.

## Current state in the library

have no counterpart.

- Injectivity and surjectivity are faithful setoid rephrasings:
